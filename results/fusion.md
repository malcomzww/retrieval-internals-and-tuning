This file is a **worked example, not a measurement**. It is a fixture

an aggregate: there is nowhere for a confound to hide.

## The setup

Document **7** is the relevant one, and *both* retrievers
find it. The dense retriever ranks it 1st; BM25 ranks it 2nd. Any sane
fusion has the evidence it needs.

| retriever | ranking (best first) | raw scores |
|---|---|---|
| dense-cosine | [7, 5, 3] | [0.86, 0.85, 0.84] |
| bm25 | [3, 7] | [30.0, 12.0] |

These score profiles are typical, not contrived. Cosine similarity is
bounded in [-1, 1] and real embeddings pack the top results into a narrow
band. BM25 is unbounded and rewards term repetition, so its spread is
wide and its scale shifts per query.

## What min-max normalisation does to them

- Dense [0.86, 0.85, 0.84] -> `[1.0, 0.5, 0.0]`. A 0.02-wide spread is
  stretched across the entire unit interval: a near-tie is now reported as
  a landslide.
- Lexical [30.0, 12.0] -> `[1.0, 0.0]`. Document
  7's genuine *second place out of two* is flattened to
  **exactly zero**, because min-max only knows the extremes of the list it
  was handed.

## The wrong answer, twice

| method | winner | relevant? |
|---|---|---|
| min-max score fusion | 3 | **no** |
| raw score fusion | 3 | **no** |
| reciprocal rank fusion | 7 | yes |

Min-max fusion sums to a tie and the tie-break hands it to the
non-relevant document. Raw fusion lets BM25's magnitude decide outright.
**Neither spelling of score fusion gets it right**, so "just don't
normalise" is not the fix.

RRF reads only ranks:

- Document 7: 1st and 2nd -> `1/61 + 1/62` = 0.03252
- Document 3: 3rd and 1st -> `1/63 + 1/61` = 0.03227

Two solid placements beat one excellent and one poor, regardless of how
emphatic any individual score was. RRF discards score magnitudes, and the
information it discards is precisely the part that was never comparable
between two retrievers on different scales.

## The judgement

Use RRF by default for hybrid retrieval. Weighted score fusion is
defensible only when both score distributions are known, stable, and
calibrated against each other -- which for BM25 is not achievable, since
its scale depends on per-query corpus statistics.

This script asserts all three winners above and exits non-zero if the
pitfall stops reproducing.
